Co-Labr

One organization, one story — how Co-Labr compares

Where Co-Labr sits next to the tools missionaries use today — Mailchimp and Epistle — and what changes when the whole organization tells its story on one platform.

Mailchimp is a general marketing tool that missionaries bend into a newsletter sender. Epistle is a genuinely good app built for the individual missionary. Co-Labr starts from a different question: what if every missionary and national staff member published from one branded home — where supporters discover the wider ministry, gifts route correctly through the organization, and leadership can actually see how their people are doing?

Mailchimp Epistle Co-Labr
Built for Businesses sending marketing email; missionaries adapt it. The individual missionary — "by missionaries, for missionaries." The whole organization. Every missionary and national staff member publishes on one platform, under the organization's own brand, with country-level co-branded pages.
Org impactOne shared home instead of hundreds of disconnected accounts — supporters, staff, and leadership all meet in the same place.
Writing an update Generic campaign builder; every sender designs their own layout from marketing templates. Quick social-style posts with photos and videos; posts bundle automatically into digest emails. A guided ministry composer: banner, photos, video, key numbers, quotes, prayer & praise, giving, and a personal sign-off. Banner and sign-off are always present, with live preview, reusable templates, and simple text formatting.
Org impactEvery update reads like ministry, not marketing — and the least design-gifted staff member still publishes something beautiful.
Branding Whatever each person builds; brand consistency depends on individual effort. Epistle's own look and feel, with the missionary's content inside it. The organization's real brand, enforced by design: block colors come from the organization's own brand palette, country pages carry national branding, and even the "most-read" highlight wears the brand color.
Org impactHundreds of communicators, one visual identity — without a communications team policing every send.
Supporter experience One-size email blasts. The supporter's only lever is unsubscribe — and it's final. Partners choose daily, weekly, or monthly digests and can like, pray, and comment from the email. Every email carries a personal preferences link: full email, quick link, monthly summary, or site-only. New supporters get a warm welcome flow; anyone leaving is gently offered a lighter option first.
Org impactFewer supporters lost at the exit door — people leave mailing lists, not relationships, and Co-Labr gives them somewhere softer to land.
Discovering the wider ministry None — each audience is a silo. A partner follows their missionary; the wider organization stays invisible. Cross-pollination is the point: a "team picks" rail on every wall, stories featured across pages with permission, the President's Monthly archive, and most-read rankings that surface the best storytelling.
Org impactA supporter who came for one family meets the whole movement — and prayer, awareness, and giving widen with them.
Giving None built in — link out and hope. Partners can give through the platform. Giving routes through the organization: every page's Give button points to the organization's designated fund for that missionary's ministry — never to an individual.
Org impactGifts stay inside the organization's own designation, receipting, and accountability systems from day one.
Member care Open and click rates. Engagement analytics — who opens, likes, prays, comments. Care Radar: AI reads across everyone's updates for hard news, big changes, new babies, and losses; sends leadership a daily care digest; flags staff who've gone quiet for 60+ days; and tracks each person's trajectory across recent updates.
Org impactLeadership learns who needs a phone call this week — from what staff actually wrote, not from open rates. No other tool in this space does this.
Helping writers improve Campaign stats: opens, clicks. Detailed engagement reporting and an activity feed. Coaching, not just counting: read-through insights, title studies drawn from real open data, most-read rankings, and AI writing insights for each page.
Org impactThe whole team's storytelling gets better over time — and great stories get seen and imitated.
Adding your people CSV imports and embedded signup forms; you manage the list mechanics yourself. Invite partners; the app requests their communication preferences. A shepherded migration: personal invite letters with one-click private links, CSV import with plain-language export instructions, and a "Your people" panel showing exactly who's following, who's deciding, and who declined.
Org impactMoving hundreds of staff off old lists becomes a guided process each missionary can do alone in an afternoon.
Data & GDPR Supporter data lives inside Intuit's marketing ecosystem. Strong security posture: encryption, private posts, invisible mode for sensitive contexts. The organization's data, under its control: each organization's list is fully isolated from every other; supporters get one-click preference changes and full self-serve erasure; US storage is disclosed and covered by EU-approved safeguards.
Org impactThe organization can answer any supporter's GDPR question — or erasure request — in seconds, not committee meetings.
Cost shape Per-missionary subscriptions that grow with each contact list — typically $20–100+/month each. $14/month per missionary (about $168/year each; 300 staff ≈ $50,000/year). One organizational platform — a single relationship covering every staff member, instead of hundreds of individual subscriptions. (Organizational pricing to be defined.)
Org impactPredictable, budgetable, org-level cost — and no staff member priced out of communicating well.
