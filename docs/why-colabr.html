Co-Labr

One organization, one story — how Co-Labr compares

Where Co-Labr sits next to the tools missionaries use today — Mailchimp and Epistle — and what changes when the whole organization tells its story on one platform.

Mailchimp is a general marketing tool that missionaries bend into a newsletter sender. Epistle is a genuinely good app built for the individual missionary. Co-Labr starts from a different question: what if every missionary and national staff member published from one branded home — where supporters discover the wider ministry, gifts route correctly through the organization, prayer actually comes back around, and leadership can see how their people are doing?

The wider landscape — at a glance

Beyond Mailchimp and Epistle, missionaries assemble their communication from a patchwork of tools. Each is good at its piece; none serves the organization. A typical missionary today runs three or four of these at once.

Tool What it is What it does well Where it stops — and where Co-Labr picks up
Prayvine Prayer-request platform, free to missionaries. Sending prayer needs to partners and gathering their responses; warm, focused, simple. Prayer only — no full updates, no giving, no organization. Co-Labr builds prayer & praise into every update, alongside the whole story.
prayerletters.com Printed prayer-letter printing & mailing service (1,000+ missionaries). Physical letters, printed, stuffed, and mailed — beloved by older supporters who want paper. Print only; the missionary still writes and manages the list elsewhere. A natural future partner: an update composed in Co-Labr could one day go out as a printed letter too.
TntConnect, Karani, DonorElf (MPDX is now Cru-only) Support-raising CRMs — donor and pledge tracking. Knowing who gives, who pledged, who to thank; the financial backbone of personal support raising. They keep the list but don't tell the story — updates still go out through Mailchimp or print. Co-Labr is the storytelling half these tools always outsourced.
Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp Social channels used informally for ministry updates. Free, immediate, where some supporters already are. The algorithm decides who sees what, the platform owns the audience, and nothing routes to prayer or giving. Co-Labr gives the org its own wall — with the list owned, not rented.

The pattern across all of them: every tool on this page — and both tools in the matrix above — serves the individual missionary. A typical staff member today runs a CRM + Mailchimp + a giving link + maybe a print service: four tools, four silos, no shared brand, and leadership sees none of it. Co-Labr is the first platform in this space built for the organization.

Mailchimp, Epistle, and the wider-landscape tools are summarized from their public websites as of August 2026 (epistle.org, mailchimp.com, prayvine.com, prayerletters.com, tntware.com); all are good tools, compared here fairly and in good faith — corrections welcome.
